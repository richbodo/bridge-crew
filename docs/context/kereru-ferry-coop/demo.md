## Spoken

With the fund's thirty-eight percent, the Coop's share comes to about three and a half million,
and it can borrow what the reserve doesn't cover without breaking the Tairua Mutual cap. The
catch is the coverage covenant. Last year ran at a small loss, so on last year's numbers no new
borrowing passes the one point two five test. The repower has to show its fuel and maintenance
savings on paper before the bank will lend. And the subsidy needs a signature before the
twenty-eighth of February, so the board can't wait for a year of data.

## Brief

```markdown
## Findings

- **The cost.** Sundstrom quotes NZD 4.31 million for the repower, plus NZD 890,000 for the
  Ōtira Point charging berth: NZD 5.20 million in all.
- **The subsidy.** The Coastal Decarbonisation Fund's 38% of the repower quote is about
  NZD 1.64 million. That leaves about NZD 3.56 million for the Coop.
- **Paying for it.** The cash reserve is NZD 2.05 million, but NZD 1.27 million of deferred
  maintenance is already queued against it. Keeping that funded means borrowing about
  NZD 2.78 million, under Tairua Mutual's NZD 3.4 million cap.
- **The covenant.** Tairua Mutual wants debt-service coverage above 1.25. Last year's operating
  surplus was −NZD 96,500, so on last year's numbers no new borrowing passes.
- **What it might save.** Sundstrom claims NZD 71,000 a year in maintenance. At 41 kWh a
  crossing, 18 crossings a day and NZD 0.1740/kWh, the *Kārearea*'s power would cost about
  NZD 47,000 a year, against its share of a NZD 2.18 million fuel bill.
- **The deadline.** The 38% only applies if the contract is signed before 28 February and the
  vessel is back in service by the end of the next calendar year. Delivery takes 14 months.

## Unknown

- The *Kārearea*'s own share of the diesel. The figures give the fleet's total only.
- Whether Tairua Mutual counts the subsidy in its coverage test.

## Confidence

Medium. The arithmetic uses the Coop's own figures. The covenant question needs Tairua
Mutual's answer.
```
