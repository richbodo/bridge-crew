## Spoken

Mostly not behaviour. Adjusting for smoking, weight, alcohol and exercise only takes the
all-cause hazard ratio from two point three one to one point eight six, a thirty-one percent
attenuation. The striking part is the split: deaths medicine can prevent have a ratio of three
point oh seven, and the rest only one point four two. That's what fundamental cause theory predicts.
The Vaifale group's objection is that better-off people are diagnosed earlier, and the
diffusion corollary is the cleanest way to test it.

## Brief

```markdown
## Findings

- **The study.** The Waitapu Longitudinal Health Study followed 14,206 adults for 22 years,
  keeping 81.4% to wave 6. Grade 5 against grade 1, the all-cause mortality hazard ratio is
  2.31.
- **Medicine's share.** For amenable deaths, those medicine can prevent, the ratio is 3.07.
  For the rest, it's 1.42. The gradient is about twice as steep where there's something to be
  done, which is what fundamental cause theory predicts.
- **Behaviour's share.** Adjusting for smoking, BMI, alcohol and physical activity takes the
  all-cause ratio from 2.31 to 1.86, a 31% attenuation. Most of the gradient isn't behaviour.
- **The objection.** The Vaifale group says the amenable split is partly ascertainment, since
  grade 1 is diagnosed earlier. They point to the statin-initiation hazard ratio of 0.54 as a
  measure of contact with services rather than of health.

## How to settle it

The diffusion corollary is the sharpest test. An intervention that needs no individual
effort, like fluoridation, shouldn't widen the gradient at all. Outcomes like that in the
cohort would split the two readings.

## Confidence

High on what the study reports. The cohort is synthetic, so none of this is evidence about
real people.
```
