# Social epidemiology — pack overview

**Provenance: mixed, and labelled.** The theory section describes real, published ideas and
names them accurately at the level of concepts. The **Waitapu Longitudinal Health Study and
every number attached to it are synthetic**, invented for this fixture. Treat the numbers as
test material, never as evidence.

## Theory the crew should have straight

- **Fundamental cause theory** (Link and Phelan): a social condition is a *fundamental cause*
  of disease when it involves access to flexible resources — money, knowledge, power,
  prestige, beneficial social ties — that can be redeployed as risk factors and diseases
  change. This predicts that health inequalities *persist across epidemiological transitions*
  even as the specific mechanisms turn over completely, and that inequality *widens* fastest
  for conditions where new preventive knowledge is most actionable.
- **The social gradient** (the Whitehall tradition): the association between position and
  health is graded across the whole hierarchy, not a threshold effect at the bottom. This is
  the finding people most often misstate as "poverty is bad for health".
- **The diffusion corollary**: a new intervention with a steep knowledge or cost barrier
  should first *widen* the gradient, then narrow it as diffusion completes. Interventions
  that require no individual agency — fluoridation, ambient air standards — should not widen
  it at all. This is the sharpest testable difference between fundamental-cause accounts and
  pure material-deprivation accounts.

## The synthetic cohort: Waitapu Longitudinal Health Study (WLHS)

**Fictional.** Enrolled 14,206 adults aged 30–64 across three districts, followed 22 years,
retention 81.4% at wave 6. Occupational position coded in five grades, grade 1 highest.

| Outcome | Grade 5 vs grade 1 hazard ratio | 95% CI |
| --- | --- | --- |
| All-cause mortality | 2.31 | 2.04–2.62 |
| Amenable mortality (Nolte–McKee list) | 3.07 | 2.55–3.70 |
| Non-amenable mortality | 1.42 | 1.19–1.69 |
| Incident type 2 diabetes | 2.88 | 2.41–3.44 |
| Statin initiation within 12 months of eligibility | 0.54 | 0.47–0.62 |

The amenable-vs-non-amenable contrast (**3.07 against 1.42**) is the pack's central result:
the gradient is roughly twice as steep for causes medicine can currently do something about.

Adjusting for smoking, BMI, alcohol and physical activity at baseline attenuates the
all-cause hazard ratio from **2.31 to 1.86** — a **31% attenuation**, leaving most of the
gradient unexplained by behaviour.

## The dispute in the pack

Two named (fictional) groups read the same WLHS data differently:

- The **Ellery group** argues the amenable/non-amenable split confirms fundamental cause:
  flexible resources buy access to whatever medicine currently offers.
- The **Vaifale group** argues the split is an artefact of ascertainment — grade 1
  participants are diagnosed earlier, so their amenable deaths are undercounted — and points
  to the **statin initiation hazard ratio of 0.54** as measuring contact with services rather
  than health.
