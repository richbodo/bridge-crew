## Spoken

Holding the floor is what makes the study possible. Bridge Crew is a research instrument first,
and what it studies is two people steering several agents at once, where there's almost no
controlled evidence. Because an agent only speaks when a person grants it the floor, every turn
is a human decision, and every one is in the record. The ten-sentence cap keeps the people in
charge, with anything longer moved into the written brief. And the predictions were published
before the first session, so the panel can see what we expect to find.

## Brief

```markdown
## The case

- **What's being studied.** Two humans steering multiple AI agents in real time on
  ill-structured problems, a setting with essentially no controlled evidence. The predictions,
  P1–P7, were published before any session ran, and they're never edited once data collection
  begins: corrections are appended.
- **The floor rule makes it measurable.** An agent works whenever it likes but speaks only when
  granted. A station is `idle`, `working`, `hand_raised`, `speaking` or `stopped`, and only one
  may be `speaking` at a time. So every turn is a human decision, and it's in the record.
- **The cap keeps the humans in charge.** A granted turn is capped at 10 sentences of spoken
  text. Anything beyond the cap moves into the written brief.
- **It can be analysed without the app.** The transcript is tagged with `context_injection`,
  `assumption_set`, `option_reopen`, `direction_change` and `convergence_break`. Every session
  exports seven files, from `manifest.json` to `corpus-manifest.json`, with no participant
  emails.

## A word to get right

Bridge Crew is the app. The Bridge Crew is everyone in the conversation, humans and agents
together.

## The strongest objection

It's slower than letting the agents talk. That's the point: the study asks what two people do
with the floor.
```
