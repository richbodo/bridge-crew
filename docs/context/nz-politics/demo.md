## Spoken

The strongest case against is what happens after the protection ends. Clause twenty-seven only
promises no worse off for three years, and Te Kaupapa Wai's own model has the average charge at
fourteen hundred and seventy dollars by year eight, about a quarter more than today. The way
back out is narrow: Clause nineteen three needs a seventy-five percent council majority and the
Minister's written consent, inside twenty-four months. A council that just tied five all won't
find nine votes. And Waitapu isn't at its borrowing cap, so the renewal programme is tight,
not impossible.

## Brief

```markdown
## The case against

- **The protection runs out.** Clause 27's "no worse off" test binds Te Kaupapa Wai for the
  first three years only. Its own model has Waitapu's average residential charge at NZD 1,061
  in year 3, under today's NZD 1,184, then NZD 1,470 by year 8, about 24% above today's.
- **There's no easy way back.** Clause 19(3) allows withdrawal within 24 months of transfer,
  and only with a 75% council majority and the Minister's written consent. On the 11-seat
  council that's 9 votes. The 14 May meeting tied 5–5, with Cr Tanira Boyd-Wolski
  abstaining.
- **The debt argument is weaker than it sounds.** Waitapu's debt-to-revenue ratio is 248%,
  against the Local Government Funding Agency's 280% cap. Funding the NZD 240 million renewal
  in-house is tight, not shut.
- **The public is against it.** Of the 2,143 submissions to the select committee, closing
  19 February, 61% opposed compulsory transfer.

## What would change this

- Ana Reti-Fong won the 7 June Motu Iti by-election, 1,912 to 1,744. Where she stands isn't
  known yet.
- A Clause 27 test that ran longer than three years.

## Confidence

High on the clauses and numbers. Low on the re-vote, which turns on two people.
```
