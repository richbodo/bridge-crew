# New Zealand regional politics — pack overview

**Provenance: a fixture briefing.** The institutions, offices and processes described here
are real features of New Zealand local government. The **council, the people, the ballot
numbers, the bill clauses and the dated milestones are synthetic**, invented so that a reader
can be caught out. Do not cite any figure in this pack as fact.

## The fight

Waitapu District Council is deciding whether to transfer its three-waters assets into a
shared regional entity, **Te Kaupapa Wai**, or to keep them in-house and fund a NZD 240
million renewal programme from rates and debt.

- The council has **11 seats**: mayor plus 10 councillors, elected by STV across three wards
  (Tautari 4, Rangi Bay 4, Motu Iti 2).
- Mayor **Piripi Ellery** (independent, elected 2025 on a 34.1% first-preference share)
  supports the transfer. The blocking bloc is five councillors led by **Deputy Mayor Susan
  Vaifale**.
- The decisive vote is **Cr Tanira Boyd-Wolski** (Motu Iti), who abstained at the
  **14 May** extraordinary meeting, leaving the resolution tied 5–5.

## The legislative hook

The **Local Water Services (Transitional Arrangements) Amendment Bill** sits at select
committee. Two clauses matter:

- **Clause 19(3)** lets a council withdraw from a shared entity within 24 months of
  transfer, but only with a 75% council majority and the Minister's written consent.
- **Clause 27** binds the entity to a "no worse off" rates test for the first three years,
  measured against each transferring council's FY-1 average residential water charge.

The committee reported back on **3 April**; submissions closed **19 February** with **2,143**
received, of which **61%** opposed compulsory transfer.

## Local numbers

- Waitapu's average residential water charge, FY-1: **NZD 1,184**. Te Kaupapa Wai's modelled
  figure for year 3: **NZD 1,061**, rising to **NZD 1,470** by year 8.
- Waitapu's debt-to-revenue ratio is **248%**, against a Local Government Funding Agency cap
  of **280%** for councils of its size.
- The Motu Iti by-election of **7 June**, triggered by Cr Halswell's resignation, was won by
  **Ana Reti-Fong** with **1,912** votes to **1,744** on the final iteration, turnout 38.6%.
